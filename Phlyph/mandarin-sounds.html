The anchor: ts and dz

An affricate is just a plosive and a fricative stacked at the same spot: block, then hiss, without moving.

You already say this — in "cats"

English never starts a word with this sound, but you make it mid-word constantly. Mandarin's z (unaspirated, like "cats") and c (aspirated, with a puff of air) just move it to the front of the word.

The three-way split

This is the contrast that trips up English speakers — three genuinely different tongue shapes English collapses into one "sh"-ish sound.

Alveolo-palatal

Tongue body flat and spread against the middle of the roof; tip stays down behind the lower front teeth.
